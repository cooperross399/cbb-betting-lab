# Decision log

Every question this build wanted to ask Cooper and answered itself, with the
answer and the reasoning, one line each. Cooper's instruction: *"That is how I
audit the autonomy afterwards without being interrupted during it. A build that
made two hundred judgment calls and recorded them is one I can review; one that
made them silently is one I have to re-derive."*

Newest last. Dates are the day the call was made.

| 1 | 2026-08-31 | The brief says the repo is `cbb-betting-lab` under `~/Projects`, but two copies of the brief sit beside it (`cbb-betting-lab-brief.md`, `cbb-betting-lab-prompt.md`). Which is `docs/original_brief.md`? | The `-prompt.md` copy. | It is the text Cooper actually sent, verbatim. | 2 | 2026-08-31 | The local `.env` in the EPL lab holds an Odds API key. Use it? | No — it is dead. | A `/v4/sports` call returned HTTP 401 `INVALID_KEY`. Proven rather than assumed, per the brief's instruction to read the environment rather than the error text. The live credential exists only as a GitHub secret, which is write-only by design and cannot be read from this machine by any means. Cooper set `CBB_ODDS_API_KEY` on the new repo unprompted at 00:40 UTC 2026-09-01, which resolves it: every credit-spending step runs in Actions, where the secret is available, and that is the architecture the brief asks for anyway. |
| 3 | 2026-08-31 | The sibling labs call their registry `leagues.py`. College basketball has no "league". | `competitions.py`, one entry, `Competition`. | The brief calls it "the competition registry" and that is the honest noun. The shape is the football lab's `League` dataclass unchanged, so the port stays mechanical. |
| 4 | 2026-08-31 | What is a game's date, when a 22:30 Pacific tip is 01:30 Eastern the next morning and 06:30 UTC the morning after that? | The **slate day**, 06:00 ET to 06:00 ET. | The NHL lab lost 69% of its bought prices to a UTC date meeting a league date, and the survivors were systematically the afternoon games. Here the exposure is worse: the late window is where the low-major games live, and the low-major end of the board is this lab's entire thesis. The boundary is not asserted — `tests/test_slate_day_matches_the_source.py` checks it against the source's own game date over every cached game. |
| 5 | 2026-08-31 | Should `Hypothesis` allow a missing predicted direction, as the football lab's does? | No — it raises. | That lab recorded the defect itself: "three of the twelve hypotheses were written with no predicted direction, so they could not be falsified by direction and three slots were spent on cuts that could only ever be exploratory". Four of its mechanisms reversed outright, and knowing they reversed was worth more than the null result — which is only sayable because a direction was written down first. |
| 6 | 2026-08-31 | The football lab's verdict for one policy flipped depending on which season had been run last. Carry that shape? | No. A verdict records `seasons_tested` and `seasons_cleared`, and `ships()` is false unless it cleared **all** of them. | Same policy, same script, opposite verdicts, and the card reads whichever ran most recently. Comparing two overlapping intervals and taking the larger number is how a lab ships noise. |
| 7 | 2026-09-01 | Which futures sport keys exist for college basketball? | `basketball_ncaab_championship_winner`, and only that. | Read from the provider's own `/v4/sports?all=true` listing rather than assumed. There is no conference-winner key and no season-win-totals key at the sport level; if those markets exist they are market keys under `basketball_ncaab`, and that is an in-season probe question rather than a September one. |
| 8 | 2026-09-01 | How is women's basketball kept out of the population at the provider? | It cannot get in: the provider serves it as a **separate sport key**, `basketball_wncaab`. | Verified in the same listing. The purity guard is therefore cheap and total on the price side — a test pins that no fetch in this repository ever names `basketball_wncaab`, `basketball_wnba` or any key but the two the registry declares. The harder half is the results side, where ESPN ids and NCAA pages do mix divisions and sexes; that is guarded separately. |
| 9 | 2026-09-01 | Cooper, mid-build: *"dont worry about women's basketball"*. Does anything change? | Nothing is built for it; the one-test purity guard stays. | It confirms the brief's scope rather than narrowing it. The guard is not a women's-basketball feature — it is a test that no fetch names a sport key the registry did not declare, and it costs one file. Removing it would not save work; it would only remove the thing that notices if a `basketball_wncaab` key or a women's ESPN id ever reaches the population by accident. |
| 10 | 2026-09-01 | CI was red on `main` for two pushes and the repo had no `tests.yml` at all. Add one that fetches the data cache so every step can run? | No. CI proves the suite, not the cache. | The cost arithmetic and the probe's dry run both read a gitignored 460MB cache. Fetching it in CI would put an external release host (sportsdataverse) on the critical path of a gate that must stay green for reasons internal to this repository — an outage there would go red here and mean nothing. Dryness is instead proved at the socket layer by `tests/test_the_dry_run_is_dry.py`, which blocks `connect()` and hides the credential, and is a stronger claim than grepping stdout for a sentence. |
| 11 | 2026-09-01 | The retention probe failed twice in Actions for missing data. Let it fall back to the raw schedule when `cbb_team_games.csv` is absent? | No. The workflow builds the table. | The probe draws its population from the same `game_state` the backtest does, precisely so the two can never disagree about which games count. A fallback would make them diverge silently — which is the class of defect that shared table exists to prevent — and it would do so in the direction that looks like success. The runner fetches and builds, and caches the result for the purchase runs behind it. |
| 12 | 2026-09-01 | The gameday workflow referenced three scripts that do not exist (`run_gameday_card.py`, `run_forward_evidence.py`, `run_what_we_can_claim.py`). How was this not caught? | Nothing had dispatched it, and no test read the workflow. Added `test_every_script_a_workflow_runs_actually_exists`. | A green workflow file is not a workflow that works — the EPL lab spent five days green and empty. The whole delivery chain sat behind three filenames that resolved to nothing, and the cheapest possible check for that is a regex over the workflow files. Written as a failing test first, then satisfied. |
| 13 | 2026-09-01 | The full historical catalogue costs 35,173,680 credits against a balance of 4,992,714. Buy a sample of everything, or all of the cheap thing? | All of the cheap thing, in the brief's stated priority order: core team markets across every season first. | Seven times the balance is not a near miss to be optimised away. The provider retains featured markets from 2020-11-16 and everything else only from 2023-05-03, so the deepest population this lab can ever have is also its shallowest catalogue: 32,379 games of moneyline, spread and total against three seasons of props. Cooper's case for a fourth lab was sample size, and the arithmetic says he is right about the team markets and wrong about the props. Recorded in `docs/credit_cost.md`. |
| 14 | 2026-09-01 | The probe's pessimistic bound is 147,020 credits — twenty times the football lab's whole probe. Cut it? | No. Run it at 144 events across all three tiers. | It is 2.9% of the remaining balance, spent to decide how the other 97% is spent, and the football lab's probe is not the comparison: that one asked "does the archive retain this market" for one population. This one asks it **per conference tier**, which is the brief's entire thesis — *"If the market's efficiency is not uniform across the board, this is where that shows up."* A probe stratified only by month would answer a question nobody asked. |
| 15 | 2026-09-01 | `scripts/run_forward_evidence.py` writes to a store that can only grow and cannot be rebuilt. Should a bare invocation settle? | No. The default is `--report-only`; settling needs `--settle`. | Same shape as `run_retention_probe.py` being dry until `--live`. There the accident costs credits; here it costs a settle pass run against a half-published box score, whose verdicts land in an append-only ledger nothing can revise. The workflow passes `--settle` explicitly, so the safe default costs nothing. |
| 16 | 2026-09-01 | A settle pass with a missing processed table or a missing cached schedule does not crash — it settles the whole night as "no game in the results tables matches". Degrade, or refuse? | Refuse, exit 2, before the first `.settled` marker is written. | It is the failure that succeeds quietly and wrongly. An empty team-name index resolves no fixture, so a night recorded that way is indistinguishable from a night that was never played — except it is permanent once the 14-day patience window closes. Note the deliberate divergence from `reports/gameday_card.py`, which falls back to an earlier season's schedule: a card can be re-made tomorrow and a settled ledger row cannot. |
| 17 | 2026-09-01 | An unreadable snapshot (the football lab's zero-byte `git show` artifact). Red run, or warning? | Named in a `::warning::`, counted in the reconciliation, exit 0. | It is a permanent fault no rerun repairs. Exiting red would mark every remaining run of the season degraded, which stops the gameday backup trigger ever standing down and spends a second slate's credits every day for a file nothing can fix. The rest of the archive settles around it, which is the brief's rule that a run degrades rather than empties. |
| 18 | 2026-09-01 | `forward_evidence.SETTLEMENT_AMBIGUOUS_MARKETS` defaults to empty — the module says deciding which of a lab's numbers are not evidence is the caller's job. What does this caller decide? | Pass the whole second-half family as settlement suspects, every run, with no flag to turn it off. | Second-half wagers settle including overtime at most US books and not all of them. That is a book's rule, this lab holds no rulebook, and those rows therefore measure the settlement rule as much as the model. Marking them **not evidence** at any sample size is the conservative direction, and a flag to unmark them would be a gate with an off switch. |
| 19 | 2026-09-01 | `forward_evidence.read_ledger` is lenient: a damaged CSV reads back as zero rows. Good enough for the report? | No. Both modes read the ledger through `stores.read_store(..., for_append=True)` and stop on `CorruptStoreError`. | The report is published to `card-feed` as `latest_forward_evidence.md`, where it is the only copy anybody reads. A damaged ledger read leniently publishes "0 frozen opinions" over a season of them and looks completely healthy — the most misleading output this lab could produce. A file that is merely absent is still zero rows, because that is a fresh clone rather than damage. |
